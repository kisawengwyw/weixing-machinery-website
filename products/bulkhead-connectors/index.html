Custom Hydraulic Bulkhead Fittings

Through-panel hydraulic bulkhead fittings manufactured to customer drawings for routing tube and hose connections through equipment panels, brackets and machine frames.

A bulkhead fitting is a through-panel mounting structure, not a separate thread or sealing standard. Its shoulder, washer and locknut primarily provide mechanical retention; the fluid seal at each end must be confirmed for that connection. A tank wall or other fluid boundary needs a separate, drawing-defined panel seal. Straight, elbow, reducing, extended-length and mixed-interface bodies can be machined to approved drawings.

Functional Separation

Mechanical Mounting vs Fluid Sealing

Mechanical mounting

  • Panel shoulder, mounting thread, washer and locknut
  • Panel thickness and mounting-hole diameter
  • Wrench access, installation direction and anti-rotation requirement

Fluid sealing

  • End 1 and end 2 connection geometry
  • Sealing face, taper thread, O-ring or flare where specified
  • Mating components, applicable sealant and system validation
The locknut retaining the body in the panel does not automatically create the hydraulic seal at either end. Locknut torque and end-connection assembly requirements are separate matters.
Installation Engineering

Panel Mounting and Fluid-Boundary Design

Structural panel

A frame, bracket, guard, cabinet or mounting plate normally requires mechanical mounting review rather than sealing of the opening.

Fluid boundary

A tank, reservoir, fluid chamber wall or wet sealed enclosure needs a separately specified panel seal. Possible drawing-defined solutions include an O-ring and machined gland, bonded seal, compatible gasket, sealing washer, welded boss, or brazed/welded penetration. Confirm seal type and surface, groove or gasket dimensions, flatness, surface condition, corrosion compatibility and specified inspection.

Loading and support

Support tube and hose routing appropriately. Do not pull misaligned lines into place or place all side load, hose bending reaction, vibration or shock on the fitting. The equipment designer must specify anti-rotation features and mounting loads when needed; no general locknut torque is given here.

Material Review

Materials and Surface Finishes

Body materials

Carbon steel and stainless steel are common choices. Brass is considered only after media, mechanical and project review; other materials are subject to drawing and application review. The specific grade must be stated by the drawing or order.

Surface finishes

Options may include trivalent zinc plating, zinc-nickel coating, black oxide, phosphating when applicable, natural stainless finish, and passivation when specified and material-compatible.

Surface finishing does not replace correct material selection, end-seal design, panel-seal design or system corrosion engineering.

Inspection Planning

Quality Control for Bulkhead Fittings

Review can cover drawing revision; both end connections; structural-panel versus fluid-boundary use; material documentation; panel thickness and hole; mounting-thread length and position; shoulder, washer seat, locknut engagement, wrench flats, hex and overall length; connection threads and sealing faces; specified O-ring grooves, taper threads and flare seats; bore, flow passage, cross-hole position, specified alignment; edge break, deburring, specified cleanliness and finish appearance.

FAQ

Hydraulic Bulkhead Fitting FAQ

What is a hydraulic bulkhead fitting?

It is a fitting body retained through a panel, bracket or frame to provide a fixed tube or hose routing point. Its end connections define fluid sealing.

Is a bulkhead fitting a thread or sealing standard?

No. Bulkhead describes a mounting structure, not an independent thread, flare, face-seal or pressure standard.

Does the locknut create the hydraulic seal?

No. The locknut primarily provides mechanical panel retention; the hydraulic seal at each end is determined by that end connection.

Can a standard bulkhead fitting seal a tank wall?

Not by default. An ordinary locknut-retained fitting is not automatically a tank-wall seal; a fluid boundary requires a separate sealing or welded structure defined by the approved drawing.

Which panel dimensions must be provided?

Provide panel thickness, mounting-hole diameter, mounting-thread length, shoulder and washer details, locknut dimensions and engagement, protrusion, access, direction and applicable anti-rotation requirement.

Which end connections can be combined?

Drawing-defined JIC 37°, ORFS, BSPP, BSPT, NPT, metric, hose and custom ends may be reviewed. Specify each end's standard, size, sex, seal, mate and assembly requirements separately.

How is the pressure capability of a bulkhead fitting determined?

It depends on both connections, size, body geometry and wall, material, seals, mating components, line assembly, media, temperature, assembly and validation plan. Panel thickness mainly affects mounting and does not directly define the hydraulic pressure rating.

What information is required to quote a custom bulkhead fitting?

Provide a drawing or verified sample, panel function and dimensions, mounting hardware, both complete end connections, body geometry, material, finish, quantity, application, validation, inspection and documentation requirements.
